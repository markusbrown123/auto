## The Witness: full entry
**Design, third pass (2026-11-27; replaces the lantern, which follows below as history).** The owner's note on the lantern: it reads as a smart speaker and is too small in a fight; wanted is something ancient, weathered, slightly lopsided, upright and angular, about half as large again, with a brighter light that turns to look.

- **What it is: the gnomon** (the upright blade of a sundial: the instrument that tells the truth by where the light falls). A tall six-sided shard of dark bronze gone brown and green, 34 cm tall, 16 wide, 12 deep at life size and drawn 1.35 times that, its top cut on a slant. Unglazed pale plates, pitted, stained and crazed, cover most of it; they are of different thicknesses, three have a corner broken off and two are missing altogether, so the bronze shows. A tall vane stands off its left side and the snapped stub of its twin off the right. A small weight hangs free under its point. No two sides are alike.
- **Its light is a lamp, and the lamp looks.** A hooded five-sided lamp is carried off-centre on the front ridge, in a dark socket. It turns by itself (62 degrees either way, 34 up and down) and reaches what the Witness looks at before the body has come round; idle, it looks about. The pane is drawn 1.7 times as bright as the lantern's bar and carries a soft bloom that fades as the lamp turns away from the viewer, so across a courtyard one can still tell where it is looking. A line of light runs under the head plates and an upright rule of light down the back shows the duel camera which way it faces.
- **It moves when it wakes or speaks.** Attending: the head plates lift 7.5 mm, the flank plates slide out, the lower plates drop, the lamp comes forward, the vanes swing out 11 to 13 degrees and the weight sinks. Speaking: head, flanks, lamp, vanes and weight all move with the voice. Idle: the head plates and the weight breathe.
- **It stays upright.** The body tips no more than 9 degrees to look up or down and banks no more than 8 into a turn; the lamp does the rest.
- **A faint trail when it darts.** Above about 1.7 m/s a thin amber ribbon follows it and fades in under half a second. Never with reduced motion.
- **It keeps out of the camera's face** in every scene: it may not come nearer the lens than the distance at which it would stand about a seventh of the picture's height (`_clear_lens`), and when a camera is watching its idle orbit is a to-and-fro sweep round the far side of the knight, never a full circle (that is what put it in the lens once a lap in the sanctuary).
- **It is not** a head, a face, a mask or an eye, and not a shell round a light: a blade with a lamp on its front. Nothing splits apart. No gold.
- **Where it comes from.** Still `tools/witness_gen.py`, variant `gnomon` (1,014 triangles, 636 in the low model), now the manifest's `_chosen`. Its three tiling textures are the tool's own: `assets/models/witness/tex/relic_*.png`. `--witnessvar=lantern` (or `plumb`, `dart`, `hexring`, `reed`, `lens`) shows an earlier one. Sheets: `marketing/witness_pass/v3/`.
- What is said below about how it roams, its states and its story still holds.

**Design (2026-11-27; replaces the mask).** The owner rejected the first Witness, a golden mask-like head at the knight's hip: too close to a well-known talking head on a belt, and "Egyptian, not clean". A first replacement, the *plumb* (a small lathe-turned ceramic teardrop with a halo), was judged too plain, "a white spinning top", and too small. The owner's reference for the FEEL is the well-known floating companion of a certain space shooter: crisp white plates, dark seams, small accents, a dark insert with a light, and free, curious movement. Its qualities were taken; its design was not.

- **What it is: the lantern.** A faceted pod about a helmet wide (23 cm wide, 15 tall, 16 deep at life size; drawn 1.12 times that), built like a product: 34 flat satin-white panels with bevelled rims standing 5.5 mm proud of a graphite core, with 2 mm dark seams between them. The crown is swept back and the keel runs forward to a small foot, so from the side it is a slanted lozenge that leans the way it looks. A gloss-black band runs round its waist. Its focal point is **one bar of warm light, off centre**: it starts left of the middle of the front and runs round the right front corner, so the thing has a side it looks from, like the index of an instrument. A short bar on the back of the band shows the duel camera where it is facing. The crown's flat and the foot are antique-gold caps.
- **It moves.** When it attends, the crown lifts 5 mm and a line of light shows all round the seam it leaves, the keel drops 4 mm and the two cheek panels slide out 5.5 mm. Idle, the crown breathes by a millimetre. When it speaks the light follows the voice and the crown and cheeks move with it. It turns its whole body to what it looks at and banks into its turns.
- **It is free.** Out of a fight it roams: a loose orbit of the knight, a look ahead down the road, a visit to each thing of interest at the stop (the ways onward and anything hidden that can be touched), where it hovers beside and above the thing and its light works over it; small hesitations; it leads when the knight walks; it comes round in front of the knight to speak, facing them. In a fight it keeps to the far edge of the ring, beyond the fighters as the camera sees them and above their heads, so it cannot cover either of them, and it answers the fight: back and away from a telegraphed blow (further from a heavy one), in close with a flare on a parry or a broken guard, over the foe when it falls, beside the knight when the knight is badly hurt, down to the knight when the knight dies. It looks at whoever is attacking. It stays inside the frame, off the pads, bars, subtitles and cards, and never on top of something that can be touched. With reduced motion: a slow orbit, no darting, no bob. `roam(false)` or `tuck(true)` hold it at the shoulder for a directed shot; a cinematic's actor has it tucked by default; `stowed()` docks it dark on the back plate.
- **States.** Dormant (dark, on its back), waking (the seam line, then the bar, then the panels part, then it rises and rights itself), free, held at the shoulder, stowed.
- **It is not** a head, a face, a mask or an eye: one bar, off centre, and nothing paired. **It is not the reference either.** That design is a compact shell of pyramid plates forming a four-pointed star around one round central lens, and it splits apart. Ours is a wide lozenge with a flat gold crown; its black silhouette has no points and no hole (`marketing/witness_pass/v2/variants_silhouettes_and_40px.png`); its light is a bar on a band, not a lens in a centre; its palette is white, graphite, antique gold and warm amber, not orange and blue; and its plates slide a few millimetres on a core, they do not fly apart.
- **Rejected in the second pass** (`marketing/witness_pass/v2/drone_variants.png`): the *dart* (three plates in an arrowhead: a paper aeroplane, and a line when seen from in front or behind, which is how a duel sees it) and the *hexring* (six wedges round a core with the light between: handsome, but a ring round a central element is the nearest thing here to the reference's shell round an eye, and it is a line from the side).
- **Where it comes from.** `tools/witness_gen.py` (parameters, no modelling package, no generation service) writes `assets/models/witness/<variant>_<part>[_lod].glb` and `witness_manifest.json`, which also says how each part is placed and moves. 1,300 triangles, 760 in the low model. `_chosen` in the manifest picks the variant; `--witnessvar=plumb` shows the first design. `src/chars/witness_drone.gd` (`WitnessDrone`) is the whole behaviour; any `FighterRig` whose look has `witness` gets one. `tools/witness_plot.py` draws its path from a log (`HR_WITNESS_LOG`). The mask, `assets/models/witness_relic.glb`, is kept on disk and unused; `--oldrelic` hangs it at the hip again.
- **Story fit.** It was found dormant behind a machined hatch at the foot of the fallen monument of the thirteen kings. It has no hands and cannot act on the world: it can see, remember, go and look, and talk. Recorded lines written for the mask that no longer fit are listed in `docs/BUG_REGISTER.md` (W-series) for the owner's decision.

**History.** See `STORY_BIBLE.md`, "The Witness". In one line: it wrote down the judgments of thirteen kings, tidied the past seventeen times when ordered, refused the eighteenth, and was walled up under their monument with the refusal cut out of its memory.

**Voice on the page.**
- Short sentences in plain old-world English. Understatement, not ornament. "Rather", "I should like", "one ought" are in its mouth; "thee", "thou", "forsooth" never are.
- Dry before grave. It makes the joke first and lets the weight arrive afterwards: "A candle three hundred years alight. In my day we called that a miracle. We were paid to."
- It indicts itself as readily as anyone: "By persons like me, in rooms like this. I merely wrote it down neatly."
- It corrects itself when a memory is not its own: "I was here. No. I was told I was here."
- It calls Lyra "the voice in the walls" until late, Orren "your smith", the player nothing at all.
- It gives the player information and opinions, never orders, and never an answer to the final choice.

**With the others.** Lyra: mutual suspicion that becomes a working respect; they agree only that "broken is not gone". Orren: his line survived by trusting nobody in a mask, and the Witness approves. The Archivist: the machine keeps what was recorded, the Witness remembers what was struck out; they have not yet been written a scene together. The Forgotten Monarch: the Witness has served "reasonable men" all its life and knows exactly what he is.

**What stays unknown.** Its name. Which of the thirteen kings was cut out of it. Who gave the order of the Great Silence, if anyone did.

**Where its lines live.** `tools/gen_dialogue.py`, the section headed "the Witness"; scene ids start `w_` (`w_<region>_<place>`, `w_<foe>_approach`, `w_<foe>_victory`, `w_talk_<topic>`) plus `witness_discovery`. Every line is marked `"recorded": false`.
