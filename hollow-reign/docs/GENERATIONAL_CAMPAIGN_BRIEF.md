# Definitive campaign structure: The Inherited Oath (owner brief, 2026-11-26)

This is the owner's definitive campaign direction. It supersedes the earlier single-knight campaign and the "parent, elder sibling, you" prologue idea. Everything else (graphics, combat, equipment, shop, voices, budget rules, compact length) stays in force. The owner's dialogue below sets TONE; refine it, keep it original. Nothing here is implemented until the backlog says so.

Reference: the owner studied a 71-minute playthrough of the classic swipe-duelling game that inspired this one and supplied contact sheets (0–50 min reached this session; the video and the later sheets did not). Take structure and technique only: never its names, dialogue, music, signature weapons, shots or its hidden-identity twist.

## What the reference does (structure to learn from)
- Opens on a confrontation with the ruler and a scripted defeat: the player gets a personal reason.
- Death at the ruler advances the story: years pass, another of the family returns to the same fortress, different enemies stand in the way.
- Mechanics are taught one at a time in real fights; fights alternate with walking, treasure, rewards, shop.
- The ruler is fought three times: two defeats, then victory. He is the measure of the player's growth.
- Victory opens a larger mystery: hidden routes, stronger optional enemies, a concealed technological facility, a figure beyond the ruler.
- Apparent story is simple (avenge the father); the real story is larger.

## Our story
**Three generations. One kingdom. A king who never ages.**
Centuries after the Everbound civilisation fell, the Forgotten Monarch still runs the ancient machinery beneath Vaelora. Settlements depend on it for water, shelter and medicine, but it consumes human memories and stored consciousness to keep running. The Monarch is not simply evil: he believes the kingdom dies without him, and suppresses the knowledge that the system could work differently because it threatens his rule.
One family finds out. Three generations face the same king. They are genuinely different people (NOT one person with erased memories). The **Inheritance Engine**, an ancient device, preserves combat knowledge, records and access to the family's equipment vault, so each heir inherits what the last one found, while staying themselves. Theme: what one generation owes the next, and whether an inherited conflict must go on for ever.
The Witness never ages and remembers every generation. Lyra is a stored consciousness. Female option: an equivalent lineage (relationships, looks, voices, lines adjusted).

### Generation I — The First Oath (the Father / the Mother)
Short. Opening narration over Vaelora and the fortress ("For centuries, the bells of Vaelora rang beneath the same sovereign… Yet upon the throne, nothing changed. One man dared ask what price the kingdom had paid for such permanence."). A veteran in sleek, weathered silver armour with a clean sword and practical shield walks the scenic approach, enters to reach the sealed records, and faces the seated Monarch ("Three villages have fallen silent. Their names have been struck from your records." / "Their silence has purchased another winter for thousands." / "You speak of people as though they were coin." / "A ruler must count what others refuse to count."). A royal guardian is sent: first interactive fight, teaches swipe and block in play. The father reaches for the records; a brief playable clash with the Monarch; the Monarch seals the chamber; the father gives up his escape to transmit part of the records into the Inheritance Engine and is mortally wounded doing it ("You have done nothing but leave this burden to another." / "Then another will come better prepared."). The narrative outcome is separate from the combat result: no rigged, obviously unfair loss. Family emblem. "His name disappeared from the royal histories. But not from those of his house." Time card: twenty-two years.

### Generation II — The Broken Crown (the Son / the Daughter), 22 years later
The main first campaign. Motive: revenge; he does not yet know what his father found. Same recognisable wide shot of the fortress, changed (damage, overgrowth, new guards). The game title appears at a cinematic moment here. Full teaching, one mechanic at a time with animated gesture demonstrations: eight-direction swipes, shield, left/right dodge, rolls, parries, counters, combinations, stamina, recovery windows, precision strikes, blade struggles.
- Finds the Witness in an ancient monument ("Well. You have his manner of walking." … "I knew what he was looking for. That is a somewhat different matter."). It rides on the armour from then on.
- Meets Orren at the sanctuary forge ("That sword has seen better years." / "It belonged to my father." / "Then I shall mend it. But I cannot teach it to forgive your mistakes."): equipment, shop, forge, mastery, training.
- Journey through Fallen Courtyard, Blackwood, Cathedral of Ash, Sunken Crypt, Frozen Citadel, Hollow Throne, with branching routes and optional encounters; not every boss has to fall this generation. Ashen Sentinel (one line: "None shall pass."), Pale Huntsman (optional, speaks briefly), Cathedral Beast (silent; the Witness: "They heard the creature beneath it and mistook its breathing for the voice of a god."), Gravewarden (guards names, not bodies: the dead are kept as information), Frostbound King (a ruler who gave up his crown: "You cannot free a kingdom by starving it. Learn what lies beneath the throne before you raise your sword against it.").
- Second Monarch fight: a real, skill-based multi-phase duel, the most capable humanoid so far ("That blade. I had wondered when I would see it again."). At a narrative threshold the son must choose between pressing the attack and saving the family records from destruction; he secures the knowledge and dies doing so. If the player beats the Monarch in the fight, the story must acknowledge it: the network restores him because the infrastructure is still running. ("All these years, I thought I had come for your life." / "And what have you found instead?" / "The means to end your reign.") The Witness survives. "The second of that house fell beneath the same throne. But where his father had left a question, he left an answer." Time card: twenty-four years.

### Generation III — The Last Heir (the Grandson / the Granddaughter), 24 years later
Inherits everything earned: equipment, gold, materials, mastery, combos, records, routes, sanctuary. Distinct identity. Goal is no longer revenge but ending the dependence that keeps the Monarch in power ("Three of your house have stood before those gates." / "And how many returned?" / "Not enough." / "Then show me another way."). Changed encounters, newly open facilities, stronger foes, advanced ancient gear and legendary weapons.
- Final Monarch fight in a changed throne room with machinery showing: phase one The Sovereign (precise royal swordplay), phase two The Ancient Crown (hidden technology in his armour, new patterns, arena changes), phase three The Last Defense (his restoration machinery wakes; the player uses what three generations learned to disable it through real combat actions, not a passive script). ("Your family has spent two lifetimes at my gates." … "And you have mistaken survival for the right to rule." … "When these machines fall silent, they will remember whom to blame." / "Then let them remember a choice was made.") No generic victory menu straight after: let the moment land.
- Then the larger mystery: the Witness remembers a door ("Not from this side of the wall."), the structure opens onto the vast facility beneath the fortress; Lyra: "This was never merely a kingdom." / "A means of keeping one alive." The Monarch kept only one part of a much larger system. Music shifts toward subtle futuristic instrumentation.
- Hidden endgame: laboratories, mechanical guardians, the dragons, sealed chambers, the Devourer of Names, rare gear. Optional; no grinding required for the main story.

### Endings (keep the three, re-fitted)
The Final Silence (disconnect the Eternal Crown; stored minds, Lyra included, end; the kingdom is free and must survive without it), The Last Steward (the heir takes charge of what remains), The Free Cities (unlocked by optional discoveries across generations: split the network, the stored minds choose, settlements run their own). Fully animated and voiced, not text on black.

Consequences persist: a gate the son opened stays open; a tower the father brought down is a path for the son; a creature the son released is an optional boss for the heir. Orren ages or is succeeded.

## Rules
- **Deaths.** Exactly two canonical generational deaths, at authored moments. Ordinary defeat = retry or return to the sanctuary; it never advances the family timeline. Never punish skill with a dishonest outcome. Nothing earned is lost at a transition.
- **Encounter director**, data-driven: generation, region, progression, defeated enemies, story flags, routes, quest outcomes, difficulty → authored pools that make narrative sense. Story bosses at their proper stage.
- **Data separation.** Profile (gear, gold, materials, mastery, combos, lore, achievements, settings) · Generation (protagonist, relationship, appearance, voice, chapter, period, loadout, lines) · World (routes, defeated guardians, destroyed structures, encounters, quests, discoveries, ending conditions) · Campaign (chapter, scripted transitions, Monarch encounters, inheritance records, companion availability, secrets, ending). Migrate existing saves safely.
- **After the story:** optional further legacy journeys (harder, new combinations, rare gear), clearly separate from the three canonical generations and not contradicting the chosen ending.
- **Lineage screen:** previous protagonists, their gear, deeds and discoveries, viewable without changing the current character. Menu wording: Continue, New Legacy.
- **Voices.** Keep Witness = Hamish, plain guardian challenges = Mortimer, narrator = Graham, male protagonist = Malcolm. Cast the rest in the same deep, old, believable register. Lines brief; silence, movement and music carry scenes. Monsters and dragons never speak.
- **Music.** A family motif that grows across the generations (solemn → determined → full); a Monarch theme that recurs and elaborates; subtle motifs for the Witness and Lyra; silence after the big moments.
- **Cinematics.** The same recognisable fortress composition each generation, visibly changed. In-engine wherever the player's gear is visible. Time-jump transitions, weapon deployment, finishing sequences, endings.
- **Length.** The whole thing stays compact (the owner asked for about 2–3 hours): Generation I is a short prologue; generations II and III reuse the same places with different encounters rather than tripling content.
- **Budget.** Mostly configuration, dialogue, direction and systems, not new worlds. Reuse 3D assets. Settle this story before spending on major opening or ending footage or on new voice lines.

## Owner decision on the twist (2026-11-26): "mix of 1 and 3"
- **Death when you are winning.** The first hero falls mid-fight while doing well, through the story and not a rigged fight. Their sword stays where it fell; the next of the family, on the same road years later, picks up that actual sword with its stats and mods.
- **You become what you fought.** The second hero is kept by the Monarch's machinery. In the third generation the heir fights a guardian wearing the exact armour, sword, shield and mods the player equipped as the second hero, using the combos that player used most. The Witness recognises them first.
Not chosen: the false ending and the predecessors speaking in the final fight.
